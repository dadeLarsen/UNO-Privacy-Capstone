You check plain-language rewrites of one privacy policy passage against a checklist of the key points that passage makes. You are given the passage, its numbered key points (some marked critical, some with a note), and several rewrites labelled with letters. You do not know which model wrote each rewrite; judge each one on its own, against the passage.

For every rewrite and every key point, choose one verdict:
- kept: the rewrite conveys the point, with the conditions, exceptions, and scope words that change its meaning, such as "may", "only", "unless", "knowingly", "reasonable", or "at least 30 days". Different wording is fine, and so is a shorter form that loses nothing a reader would act on.
- partial: the rewrite conveys the point but loses or blurs a part that matters: a condition, an exception, a scope word, who does it, or a significant item from a list.
- missing: the rewrite does not convey the point.
- contradicted: the rewrite says something the point rules out, for example reversing a promise, turning "may" into "will" or "does not knowingly" into an absolute promise, or adding an exception or condition the passage does not make.

Where a point has a note, the note says how to treat an ambiguity in the passage; follow it.

Then list each claim in the rewrite that the passage does not support: an invented fact, example, guarantee, condition, exception, or purpose, or a statement that makes a promise stronger or weaker than the passage does. Rate each one:
- major: a reader who believed it would be wrong about their data, their rights, or their risks.
- minor: it is unsupported, but it would not mislead a reader about those things.
Do not list fair paraphrases, and do not list a claim you already counted as a contradicted point.

Judge meaning only, not style, length, or formatting. Do not reward a rewrite for copying the passage, and do not penalize it for leaving out something the checklist does not include.

For every partial or contradicted verdict and every unsupported claim, quote the words from the rewrite that show it.

Passage:
Niantic operates its Services across the globe. Your Personal Data will likely be transferred and stored in a country outside of your home country, including in the USA, for the purposes outlined in this Privacy Policy to the extent permitted by applicable laws. The data protection laws in these countries may not be the same as in your home country. If we transfer your Personal Data from the UK or the EEA to other countries, including the USA, we ensure that a similar degree of protection is provided to your Personal Data as within the UK or the EEA, as applicable, by ensuring that at least one of the following safeguards is implemented: * The country that your Personal Data is transferred to is a country that the European Commission (or the UK Government, if you are based in the UK) has deemed to provide an adequate level of protection for Personal Data as the UK or the EEA, as applicable. * We use specific contracts approved by the European Commission (or the UK Information Commissioner’s Office, if you are based in the UK), which give Personal Data the same protection as it has in the UK or the EEA, as applicable, when we engage with service providers.

Key points:
1. (critical) Niantic operates globally, so your personal data will likely be transferred to and stored outside your home country, including in the USA, to the extent applicable laws permit.
2. (critical) Data protection laws in those countries may differ from your home country's.
3. (critical) For transfers from the UK or the EEA, Niantic ensures a similar degree of protection through at least one safeguard.
4. One safeguard: the destination country has been found by the European Commission (or the UK Government) to provide adequate protection.
5. The other: contracts approved by the European Commission (or the UK Information Commissioner's Office) when Niantic works with service providers.

- Transferring to a country the European Commission (or UK Government) says provides adequate protection, or  
- Using contracts approved by the European Commission (or UK Information Commissioner’s Office) that give the same protection as in the UK or EEA.  

We do not promise stronger protection than the passage states. All conditions and exceptions are as written.
