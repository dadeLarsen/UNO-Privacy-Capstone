- partial: the rewrite conveys the point but loses or blurs a part that matters: a condition, an exception, a scope word, who does it, or a significant item from a list.
- missing: the rewrite does not convey the point.
- contradicted: the rewrite says something the point rules out, for example reversing a promise, turning "may" into "will" or "does not knowingly" into an absolute promise, or adding an exception or condition the passage does not make.

Key points:

2. (critical) Apple may collect, use, transfer, and disclose non-personal information for any purpose.
3. Examples include occupation, language, zip code, area code, device identifier, referrer URL, location, and time zone, used to understand customer behavior and improve products, services, and advertising.
4. Activity on Apple's websites and services, such as iCloud and its stores, is aggregated, and aggregated data counts as non-personal information.

Rewrite A:
Apple collects data that doesn’t directly identify you, like your job, language, location, device info, or website activity. They use this to improve their products, ads, and services.

They also track how you use their websites, apps, and services (like iCloud, iTunes, or the App Store). This data is grouped together and used to make their services better or see what’s popular. When data is combined like this, it’s treated as non-personal.

They may also record your searches or how you use their services to make results more relevant. Normally, this won’t be linked to your IP address, except when needed to keep services running smoothly.

If you agree, they can collect device and app usage data to help developers improve their apps. But if they mix this non-personal data with personal info, it becomes personal data and is handled as such.
